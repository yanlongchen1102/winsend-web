Copy and Paste Between iPhone and Mac Not Working? Fixes and a Different Apple ID Workaround

You copy something on your iPhone, press ⌘V on your Mac, and nothing happens. Here is what Apple's copy and paste between devices needs, how to fix it step by step, and what to do when your iPhone and Mac use different Apple IDs.

Last updated: October 31, 2026

Quick Answer

Copy and paste between an iPhone and a Mac is an Apple feature called Universal Clipboard. It only works when both devices are signed in with the same Apple Account (Apple ID), are near each other, and have Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and Handoff turned on. Checking those four things fixes most cases.

If your iPhone and Mac are signed in with different Apple IDs, for example a work Mac and a personal iPhone, Universal Clipboard cannot work at all. Use an app that copies over your local network instead, such as WinSend for Mac.

What Copy and Paste Between iPhone and Mac Needs

According to Apple's Universal Clipboard guide, it works when your devices are set up like this:

  • Same Apple Account on the iPhone and the Mac;
  • Bluetooth and Wi-Fi turned on on both devices;
  • Handoff turned on on both devices (it is on by default);
  • The devices are near each other.

Copied content is only kept on the other device briefly, or until you copy something else, so paste soon after you copy.

How to Fix Copy and Paste Between iPhone and Mac

Work through these steps in order and test after each one: copy a short piece of text on the iPhone, then press ⌘V on the Mac.

  1. Check the Apple Account. On the iPhone, open Settings and tap your name at the top. On the Mac, open System Settings and click your name. Both must show the same Apple Account. If they don't, skip to different Apple IDs.
  2. Turn on Bluetooth and Wi-Fi on both devices. The devices don't need to be on the same Wi-Fi network for this check, but both radios must be on.
  3. Turn on Handoff. On the iPhone: Settings > General > AirPlay & Continuity (or AirPlay & Handoff) > Handoff. On the Mac: System Settings > General > AirDrop & Continuity (or AirDrop & Handoff) > “Allow Handoff between this Mac and your iCloud devices”.
  4. Turn Handoff off and on again on both devices if it was already on.
  5. Keep the devices close, and paste within a short time after copying.
  6. Install software updates on the iPhone and the Mac.
  7. Restart both devices.
  8. Still not working? As a last resort, sign out of your Apple Account on one device and sign back in. Make sure you know your password and that your data is backed up first.

Using a company Mac? Your IT department may manage which Continuity features are allowed. If Handoff is unavailable or greyed out, ask your administrator.

Why It Can't Work with Different Apple IDs

Universal Clipboard is tied to your Apple Account: Apple only passes copied content between devices signed in to the same account. No setting changes that. Common situations where this gets in the way:

  • A work Mac signed in with a company Apple Account, and your personal iPhone;
  • A family Mac signed in with someone else's Apple ID;
  • You want to copy something to a colleague's or partner's Mac.

Switching the Mac to your personal Apple ID is often not possible or not wanted. In those cases you need a tool that does not depend on Apple ID.

How to Copy and Paste Between iPhone and Mac with Different Apple IDs

WinSend connects your iPhone and Mac over your local network instead of your Apple Account, so it works whatever Apple ID each device uses. Copy text on one device and paste it on the other; images can be sent too.

Set up once

  1. Download WinSend for Mac (macOS 13 or later) from the download page, open the DMG and drag WinSend to Applications. It runs in the menu bar.
  2. Install the WinSend app on your iPhone from the App Store.
  3. Connect both devices to the same local network and allow Local Network access when asked. Your Mac then appears in the WinSend app on the iPhone.

From iPhone to Mac

  1. Copy text or an image on the iPhone as usual.
  2. Open WinSend and tap Send to PC. The button says PC, but it sends to whichever computer you connected, including your Mac.
  3. Press ⌘V on the Mac.

From Mac to iPhone

  1. Copy text or an image on the Mac with ⌘C.
  2. On the iPhone, open WinSend and tap Get from PC.
  3. Paste the text on the iPhone. A copied image is saved straight to Photos.

Faster: use a Shortcut or Back Tap

WinSend adds two actions to the Shortcuts app: Send Clipboard to PC and Sync Clipboard From PC. Both work with your Mac. Add them from the Shortcuts setup page in the WinSend app, then assign one to Back Tap (Settings > Accessibility > Touch > Back Tap). After that, copy on the iPhone, double-tap the back of the phone, and paste on the Mac, without opening WinSend. The step-by-step Shortcut setup is in the copy and paste guide.

Good to know

  • Clipboard copying runs over your local network. Use a trusted private network: local transfers are not end-to-end encrypted.
  • Optional Cloud Sync can send clipboard text when the iPhone and Mac are on different networks. Images and files still need the local network.
  • WinSend on the Mac skips content that password managers mark as concealed, so copied passwords are not sent.
  • The same app also sends photos and files between iPhone and Mac, in both directions.
  • The Mac app is free. Transfers use the iPhone app's one-time free allowance, which covers every feature; after that, WinSend Pro is a one-time purchase on iPhone with no usage limits.

What About AirDrop?

AirDrop is different. It does work between devices with different Apple IDs: set the receiving device to Contacts Only (if you are in its contacts) or Everyone for 10 Minutes. For sending a photo or file once in a while, AirDrop is fine.

But AirDrop is not a clipboard. It can't put copied text on your Mac so you can press ⌘V, and it doesn't help when you move snippets back and forth all day. That is the gap WinSend fills.
